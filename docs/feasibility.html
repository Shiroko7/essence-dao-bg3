01 / SHAPEThe system, and why BG3 already fits it

Your system is: a pool of points that scales with level, spent on talents drawn from nine themed trees, where passives permanently bind part of the pool and actives spend from it temporarily.

BG3 ships that exact shape already. The Sorcerer has SorceryPoint — a custom resource with its own replenish rule — and Metamagic, a set of passives chosen from a list that modify spells and charge points on cast. Every mechanism the Essence system needs is load-bearing in shipped content, which means it is tested, works in multiplayer, and survives save/load.

The pool follows your formula, level + 4 per tier threshold crossed, and lands at 24 points at BG3's level 12 cap. Initiate, Adept and Master are all reachable; Grandmaster and Great Grandmaster are not, so their content is simply not built.

03 / SCOPEWhat got cut, and why

166 entries are out. The rule applied was simple: if BG3 has no way to represent the effect, it does not ship. Nothing here is a rebalance — these are things the engine has no vocabulary for.

141 spells with no BG3 implementationCut · 141

I parsed all 3,322 spell entries in the game and matched them against your lists. Only 91 have a usable implementation, and those are the ones that ship — referenced directly, so they come with Larian's icons, VFX and tooltips for free.

The cut list includes Meteor Swarm, Time Stop, Prismatic Spray, Wall of Force, Antimagic Field, Earthquake, Control Weather, Fabricate, Animate Objects, Foresight, Tiny Hut, Sword Burst, Magic Stone, Mold Earth, Frostbite, Green-Flame Blade, Create Bonfire and Lightning Lure. Each would be a full authoring job — stats entry, icon, VFX, animation — for a spell the game has never had.

25 custom abilities that rest on systems BG3 lacksCut · 25

Artisan tools (mason's, smith's, woodcarver's, navigator's) do not exist. Neither does swimming, underwater movement, weather, moonlight, gemstone appraisal, mile-range messaging, or speaking with elementals and plants.

The largest single casualty is Vital Essence Sublimation I–III, which needs a crafting subsystem that converts an arbitrary monster's ability into a potion. That is not a feature; that is a second mod. Sky-Piercing Execution also goes — it triggers on a creature being airborne, a state BG3 does not model, and its source text is unfinished anyway.

16 abilities ship with a clause removedAdjusted · 16

These keep their core effect but lose an unrepresentable qualifier. Stone Camouflage becomes flat Stealth expertise rather than expertise "against stone surfaces"; Draconic Regeneration drops the moonlight requirement; Ascending Dragon Gale keeps its thunder damage, Restrained and Prone but loses the 20-foot launch. Each adjustment is listed on its row in the triage below.

Three source-data bugs to fix upstreamData

Liberation's Gale has Immovable Mountain's paragraph pasted onto the end of its description. Sky-Piercing Execution literally contains [DESCRIPTION INCOMPLETE]. Earthen Ward is tagged tier: active, which is not one of your five tiers. Worth fixing in the markdown before I build against it.

04 / INTERFACEWhere the tree actually lives

You were right to reject the dialogue tree — 122 talents behind conversation nodes would be miserable. Chasing the alternative turned up one hard no and one very good yes.

Adding to a race additively is not supportedAnswered · no

I checked all 982 progression rows across the three shipped progression files. Same table, same level, two rows is a pattern that never occurs in a single context. The 27 apparent cases all turned out to be multiclass variants — the second row carries IsMulticlass true and the engine picks one row per context, it does not concatenate them.

So "add on top of race features" would in practice mean shipping a rewritten copy of each race's rows with your selectors appended. That is 29 race tables, it breaks any other mod touching races, and modded races silently get nothing. Exactly what you wanted to avoid.

BG3 already ships the tree you are describingAnswered · yes

The Illithid powers panel is driven by TadpolePowersTree.lsx — a plain data file of 26 nodes, each naming a spell or passive, each with an optional list of PrerequisiteUUIDs. It is a prerequisite graph with its own UI page, its own currency, and no relationship to class or race whatsoever. That is your talent tree, feature for feature.

The catch: the file declares one region with one root, and there is no tree identifier. The game reads exactly one tree. A mod can add nodes to the Illithid panel, but cannot open a second panel beside it from data alone.

To be unambiguous: the Illithid tree is not touched. TadpolePowersTree.lsx is never opened, no nodes are added to it, and tadpole powers behave exactly as they do in an unmodded game. What we take from it is the shape — a node list with a prerequisite graph — copied into a file of our own.

A custom panel cannot work on macOSPlatform

Norbyte's Script Extender is a Windows DLL. macOS is served by a community reimplementation, tdimino/bg3se-macos, which is genuinely active — commits this week, MIT licensed — but sits at v0.42 and, decisively, does not implement Ext.IMGUI at all. Its API table covers 22 namespaces and IMGUI is not among them; Ext.UI/Noesis is listed as excluded by explicit decision, stub layer only.

So a bespoke Essence window is a Windows-only feature. There is no version of this where a custom panel renders on a Mac.

Everything except the panel is fine cross-platformGood news

The same port reports high parity on precisely the namespaces the mod's logic needs: Ext.Osiris, Ext.Stats (96.2% behavioural), Ext.Entity, Ext.Vars for persistence, and Ext.StaticData — which explicitly covers Progression and ActionResource.

The split is therefore clean: shared logic, platform-specific front end. Point accounting, tier gating, breakthroughs and every ability work identically on both. Only the way you browse and pick talents differs.

Decided: the spellbook is the picker

Each essence becomes a container in your spellbook — nine of them, grouped under Cultivation, which is its own section in practice. Opening one lists that path's techniques; casting one learns it, permanently.

The reason this needs no scripting is that BG3 does the enforcement itself. A technique carrying UseCosts "EssencePoint:2" greys itself out when you cannot afford it, and one gated on HasPassive stays locked until you hold its prerequisite — or its breakthrough pill. The budget cannot be cheated because the engine, not the mod, is counting. Single-level containers are proven across 96 shipped examples; it is only nesting them that Larian never does.

It cannot draw the prerequisite graph, which is the one real loss. In exchange the mod has no dependencies at all — it runs on Windows and Mac, cannot break when Script Extender lags a patch, and stays installable by anyone.

The last piece is how the pool grows without a progression table, and Osiris answers it: LeveledUp(_Character) is a real event — Larian uses it to refill Portent — so the mod recomputes your pool on level-up and grants it as a passive. SavegameLoaded covers characters who existed before the mod was installed.

05 / BREAKTHROUGHFoundation and Golden Core

Fully feasible, and better than you proposed — BG3's alchemy is real, data-driven and additive, so the pills can be genuinely brewed rather than bought. All three of your acquisition routes work, so I would ship all three.

The gate is structural, not a condition check

Each tier's nine path containers are unlocked by that tier's passive — the Foundation pill literally carries UnlockSpell for the nine Adept containers. Without the pill those containers do not exist on your character, so an over-tier technique cannot be browsed, learned, or cast. There is no cast-time condition to get wrong and no way to hold a technique you should not have.

It also means your spellbook visibly grows at each breakthrough: nine Cultivation entries become eighteen, then twenty-seven. The advancement is something you can see, not just a number that moves.

Why real alchemy works

BG3 ships 126 alchemy recipes as ordinary stats entries — an ItemCombination naming up to three ingredients and an ItemCombinationResult naming the output. Stats entries are additive by name, so new recipes appear in the alchemy UI without overwriting anything. Here is a shipped one, and it is the entire pattern:

new ItemCombination "ALCH_Potion_Healing_RoguesMorsel" data "Object 1" "ALCH_Extract_RoguesMorsel" // an extract data "Object 2" "ALCH_Affinity_Water" // a reagent category data "AlchemyCombinationType" "ExtractToSolution"

Better still, BG3's reagents are already sorted into affinity categories — Air, Earth, Fire, Water, Fey and Shadow. Four of those are your essences. A Foundation Establishment Pill built from an Earth-affinity reagent reads as though Larian designed the shelf for it.

The three routes, all cheap once the item exists

  • Brew it. A recipe using existing world reagents, discoverable in the alchemy UI like any other. The intended path.
  • Bring the reagents to Withers. He brews it for you, for a fee. Covers players who never engage with alchemy, and it is only a dialogue branch plus an item swap.
  • Buy it outright. Expensive enough to sting. The escape hatch so nobody is hard-blocked from the content they installed the mod for.

Mechanically the pill is a consumable applying a permanent status that carries a Passives field — the same construction as BG3's own elixirs. That passive is then a prerequisite node in the tree, so the gate is enforced by the same graph that handles every other prerequisite. No special-casing.

One design note worth raising now: gating the pool as well as the talents makes the fiction land harder. If the pool simply stops growing without a breakthrough, a player who ignores it feels the ceiling in every fight rather than only on the level-up screen — which is precisely what a stalled cultivator is supposed to feel.

06 / VOICEHow he ought to sound

A Daoist senior appraising a talentless pupil, in Withers' register: archaic, unhurried, second-person singular, no contractions. He is not impressed, and he is not unkind. Draft lines for the opening exchange —

Thou standest within a current thou hast never once perceived, and namest the stillness thine own doing. It is… a talent. Not the one thou wouldst have chosen.

I want to learn the Dao.

Thou wantest. Naturally. The wanting is very nearly the whole of thee.

Attend. There are nine breaths beneath the world, and thou art deaf to all nine. This is no failing. A stone does not fail to sing. But thou hast come to me, and I have buried a great many who came to me, so let us begin where beginners begin.

What do I have to do?

Less than thou fearest. More than thou wilt enjoy. Choose a breath. Bind it. It shall cost thee something thou wilt not get back — that is what binding is. Mortals forget this and are surprised, always, at the end.

And if I want to stop?

Then thou shalt tell me so, and I shall unmake it, and thou shalt be as thou wert. I am not thy master. I am merely the one who remains after the masters are done.

And when you ask him to brew it for you

Brewing it yourself is the intended path, so his line for doing it on your behalf should cost you a little dignity —

Could you prepare the pill for me?

…I could.

Give them here. Earth-salt, and a root pulled before the frost. Thou hast carried these across half a realm without once wondering what they wanted to become.

There. A Foundation Establishment Pill. It took the length of thy breathing.

Thou couldst have done this. That is the part I would have thee sit with. Not that thou didst not — that thou couldst have, and did not think to try.

The taunt is doing real design work: it tells the player the recipe exists, that they already had the reagents, and that brewing is the intended path — without a tutorial popup. If they come back a second time he should be shorter about it, and by the third simply hold out his hand.

The bottleneck

Gated on EssenceDao_State_BottleneckFoundation — level 5 or beyond with no Foundation. The option only appears once the player is genuinely stuck, so it reads as them noticing, not as a quest marker.

Something is wrong. I have not advanced in some time.

No. Thou hast not.

Thou art a vessel filled to its brim, and thou keepest pouring. Where didst thou imagine it was going?

Then how do I go further?

Thou dost not go further. Thou goest deeper. The vessel must be made larger before it can be filled again — that is a Foundation, and it is established with a pill, and the pill is made of things that grow in the ground.

Earth-salt. A root pulled before the frost. Thou hast walked past both a hundred times.

And if I bring them to you?

Then I shall do for thee what thou couldst do for thyself, and we shall both know it.

The Golden Core version at level 9 runs the same shape with less patience — he has explained this once already, and says so.

The last wall

Gated on EssenceDao_State_AtTalentLimit — level 12 with both breakthroughs taken. There is no third pill, and there never will be. This is also the mod's in-fiction cover for BG3's level cap, which is a better answer than a greyed-out button.

I am ready for the next breakthrough.

Art thou.

…No. Thou art at the end of what thou art. There is a third gate. Thou wilt not pass it, and I shall not insult thee by pretending the fault lies in thy preparation.

Then make me a pill for it.

I have made that pill. Twice, for others. Both died with the taste still on their tongues, and they were better than thee.

Thou hast come a long way on a small talent. That is rarer than a large one, and I would not have thee waste the ending of it clawing at a door that was never thine.

Go. Thou art as much as thou shalt be. It is enough — which is not the same as saying it is much.

Worth keeping this line dry rather than cruel. He is not mocking the player; he has simply outlived enough of them to have stopped rounding up. The sting lands harder for being accurate.

The register does two useful jobs. It sells the fiction, and it lets the tutorial text be genuinely instructive without breaking character — "it shall cost thee something thou wilt not get back" is the passive-binds-your-pool rule, stated in his voice.

08 / BUILDOrder of work

Nothing is open. Every mechanism is verified against shipped game data, and the mod needs no dependencies and overwrites nothing.

Then, in order

  • The spine. Essence resource, per-level growth, and the Withers dialogue that switches the system on and off. Verifiable in-game the same day; everything else hangs off it.
  • The tree data and picker. Node-plus-prerequisites file of our own, plus whichever front end you pick below. The budget is enforced by UseCosts either way, so it cannot be cheated.
  • The two breakthroughs. Both pills, their alchemy recipes, and Withers' brew-for-a-fee and buy-outright branches.
  • The 92 drop-in talents. Grade A actives and passives — mostly one-line boosts and standard damage-plus-save spells.
  • The 91 reused spells. Referenced from existing entries, so they arrive with Larian's icons, VFX and tooltips.
  • The 38 grade-B builds. Interrupts, summons, auras, surface trails. This is where the real time goes.
  • The 16 adjusted abilities, each shipping with its simplification stated in the tooltip so it reads as intentional.

One balance note for once it is playable: the trees are lopsided. Poison ships 30 entries against metal's 11, so metal will feel thin at Adept and above. Worth either thinning poison or writing a few more metal talents before release.
